Hackathon Theory Architecture

Behavioral Science & DIKW

Concept & Architecture by @Tsz Ho Wong

1. The DIKW Hierarchy

Raw telemetry like 58.2 MPG sits at the base of Ackoff's DIKW pyramid without meaning.

  • Data: 58.2 MPG captured over 490.1 miles.
  • Information: "+14.2 MPG vs class" & "Top 8% in Greater London".
  • Knowledge: Saved £48.20 and avoided 38.4kg CO₂ this cycle.
  • Wisdom: "Pulse & Glide" on the A40 roundabout descent.

2. Don Norman's Gulf of Evaluation

Drivers traditionally look at 58.2 MPG and face a high cognitive gulf: "Is this good? Did I drive well? What should I do next?"

By pairing the MPG instantly with an intuitive percentile badge and monetary savings, the Gulf of Evaluation is closed in less than 500 milliseconds.

3. Social Comparison (Festinger)

Humans evaluate their performance by comparing with relevant peers. Benchmarking against identical models (Toyota Yaris Cross) prevents false comparisons against full EVs or diesel lorries.

4. Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)

  • • Autonomy: Interactive savings simulator lets users define their own target.
  • • Competence: Progress bars, 85/100 score, and achievable badges.
  • • Relatedness: Community total: "142 tonnes saved together in London".

5. Fogg Behavior Model (B = MAP)

Instead of generic advice ("Drive better"), the app gives:

Motivation: £578/yr saved + CO₂ cuts.
Ability: Tiny frictionless action (Lift off accelerator 200m before roundabout).
Prompt: Geo-targeted context on your usual A40 route.
